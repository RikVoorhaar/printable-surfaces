Printable Surfaces — Blog Post Plan

Writing plan for the post about support-free lattices in the Platonic solids. Seven sections, each with two human gates. This file records structure, decisions and gate status — never drafted prose, and never section contents that have not been agreed out loud first.

Writing contract

How this works

  1. One section at a time. No drafting a section before the previous one is approved. No exceptions, no "while I'm here" drafts.
  2. Two gates per section. Gate A: we agree, in rough bullets, what goes in the section. Gate B: the draft is written and reviewed rendered in the browser.
  3. Recall before grilling. When a section opens, the agreed bullets are restated back before any discussion of the draft, so the context is on the table.
  4. The draft is the only prose. It lives in blog/src/posts/3d_print_platonic_solids.mdx and is reviewed at http://100.81.11.110:4321/blog/3d_print_platonic_solids. This plan holds titles, bullets, status and open questions only.
  5. Nothing is recorded as agreed until it is agreed. A section's contents box stays empty until we have actually talked it through.
  6. The budget is fixed. If a section wants more words, another one gives them up. The post does not grow.
  7. Figures belong to the section that uses them. Chosen at gate A, produced before gate B, reviewed with the draft.

Facts to keep straight

Pulled from the code, because the story is easy to mis-remember.

FactTruth
Printability rule A rod is printable if its axis is at ≥ 48° elevation, or it is a horizontal bridge ≤ 10 mm; rods lying on the build plate are exempt. This is not the solid-overhang (“face normal”) rule.
Stretch Dodecahedron is the stretched one: face-down, ×1.93 vertical. Icosahedron is vertex-down, not uniformly stretched — its tip pyramids are lifted locally so the tip edges reach 50°.
No stretch Tetrahedron, cube and octahedron are regular, face-down, no stretch. (Vertex-down octa would have needed ×1.19 — that is why it is face-down.)
Pipeline Place (frame + constructive face fill + uniform interior) → evolve (force relaxation with support pull, 1/r² repulsion, anchor) → choose (MIP over candidate rods) → repair (degree 3, flat horizontals, upward links).
Coulomb gas It is a force term in the relaxation, not the sampling method. It gets a figure, not a section.
Where it hurts The horizontal top face: a ceiling of bridges with nothing below it. That is the real narrative turn, not “the icosahedron needs stretching”.
Still imperfect 14 cube / 4 octahedron nodes have no printable upward link (the layer under the ceiling is ~5.6 mm too low); top-face hatching has rods longer than the 10 mm bridge limit.
